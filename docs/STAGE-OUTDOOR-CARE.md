# UltraRig Outdoor Stage, Heat & Environmental Care Guide

Performing on outdoor festival stages, open-air beach clubs, and daytime wedding ceremonies presents severe environmental challenges that indoor venues do not. 

Under direct summer sunlight, the dark extruded aluminum chassis of the M-VAVE Tank-G and Tank-B acts as a solar heat sink. Combined with the continuous processing heat generated by real-time NAM A2-Lite neural network inference and 1024-point convolution, enclosure temperatures can quickly exceed **55°C (131°F)**.

Excessive heat can trigger **DSP thermal throttling** (causing audio stuttering or dropouts), accelerate **lithium-ion battery degradation**, and wash out stage visibility.

This guide provides practical field techniques for heat dissipation, battery safety, sun-glare visibility, and protection against wind-blown dust and static discharge.

---

## 1. Thermal Management & Dissipation Mechanics

```
 [ DIRECT MIDDAY SUNLIGHT (~1,000 W/m² Solar Radiation) ]
                          │
                          ▼
            ┌───────────────────────────┐
            │ Dark Aluminum Top Chassis │ ──> Reaches 50°C - 58°C in direct sun
            ├───────────────────────────┤
            │ Embedded DSP Microchip    │ ──> Continuous NAM A2-Lite Math (+10°C internal)
            ├───────────────────────────┤
            │ Internal Li-ion Battery   │ ──> Sits directly against chassis bottom
            └───────────────────────────┘
                          │
                          ▼
 [ BOTTOM CHASSIS PASSIVE DISSIPATION: MUST HAVE FREE AIRFLOW ]
```

### The Aluminum Chassis is Your Heatsink:
The Tank-G has no internal cooling fan; it relies entirely on passive thermal conduction through its aluminum alloy enclosure to shed heat into the surrounding air.

### Critical Airflow Rules:
1. **Never Enclose in Foam During Performance:** Never operate the pedal inside a closed flight case, pedalboard foam cutout, or soft gig bag under the sun.
2. **Avoid Insulating Carpets:** Placing the pedal directly onto thick synthetic stage carpeting, towels, or blankets blocks airflow beneath the bottom plate, trapping heat.
3. **Use Elevated Risers / Rubber Feet:** Ensure at least **10 mm (0.4 inches)** of open air space beneath the pedal's bottom plate to promote natural convective cooling.

## 2. Lithium-Ion Battery Stage Safety

```
 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                    BATTERY THERMAL THRESHOLDS                          │
 ├───────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ 0°C to 45°C       │ Safe Operating & Fast Charging Temperature         │
 │ 45°C to 55°C      │ Safe for Discharge (Playing); DO NOT CHARGE        │
 │ Above 55°C        │ Thermal Protection Shutdown; Battery Wear Risk     │
 └───────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> [!CAUTION]
> **The Golden Outdoor Rule: NEVER charge the pedal while playing an outdoor gig in direct sunlight.**
> Charging a lithium-ion battery generates internal chemical heat. If the pedal is already hot from direct sun and heavy DSP compute, plugging into a high-current USB charger can push internal battery temperatures past the safe 50°C threshold.

### Proper Outdoor Charging Strategy:
* **Fully charge the pedal indoors** in an air-conditioned room or shaded green room prior to your set.
* On stage, disconnect the USB charger and run the pedal exclusively on its internal battery. Operating on battery power produces significantly less internal heat.

## 3. Passive Shading Tricks for Direct Sunlight

If your stage has no roof canopy and the pedal is exposed to midday sun:

```
                  [ Direct Sun ]
                        │
                        ▼
      ┌───────────────────────────────────┐
      │   White Cardboard / Gaffer Shade  │ ──> Reflects 85% of solar radiation
      └───────────────────────────────────┘
                        │
                        ▼ (Cooled Shadow Zone)
            ┌───────────────────────┐
            │  Tank-G / Tank-B Unit │
            └───────────────────────┘
```

1. **The White Gaffer Tape Strip:** Apply a strip of white matte gaffer tape across the flat top portion of the chassis (leaving knobs, LEDs, and footswitches uncovered). White tape reflects up to 85% of radiant solar heat, dropping chassis temperatures by **8°C to 12°C**.
2. **The Stage Wedge Shadow:** Position your pedalboard directly inside the cast shadow of a stage monitor wedge, vocal mic stand, or amplifier cab.
3. **The Cardboard Sun Hood:** Keep a folded piece of white or light cardboard in your gig bag to place over the pedal during soundchecks and intermission breaks.

## 4. Sunlight Glare, Screen & Polarized Glasses Solutions

Direct sunlight washes out LED rings and mobile device displays:

### Polarized Sunglasses "Blackout"
* Many smartphone and tablet screens use polarizing filters. When viewed through polarized sunglasses, the screen may appear completely black when held at certain angles.
* **The Fix:** If your tablet screen turns black, rotate the device **90 degrees** (e.g., from portrait to landscape) to align with your glasses' polarizing angle.

### Editor Daylight Mode
* Open the UltraRig Web Editor on your phone or laptop.
* In high-glare environments, click **Settings** → **High-Contrast Stage Display**.
* The UI switches to high-contrast monochrome with bold neon indicator outlines, making bank numbers, active scenes (A/B/C/D), and the chromatic tuner clearly legible from standing height.

## 5. Outdoor Environmental Hazards: Dust, Salt & Rain

```
 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                      ENVIRONMENTAL DEFENSE                             │
 ├───────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Beach Salt Air    │ Wipe metal jacks with contact cleaner after gig    │
 │ Wind-blown Sand   │ Protect 3.5mm and USB-C ports with silicone plugs │
 │ Carpet Static     │ Ensure audio cable is grounded through PA snake    │
 │ Sudden Rain       │ Keep heavy transparent plastic wrap in gig bag     │
 └───────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Ocean & Beach Gigs (Salt Fog Corrosion):**
   - Salt-laden ocean air rapidly oxidizes exposed brass contacts inside the 6.35 mm input/output jacks and USB-C connector.
   - After coastal gigs, blow out the jacks with compressed air and insert/remove a 1/4" plug treated with DeoxIT contact cleaner to wipe away salt deposits.
2. **Dust and Sand Protection:**
   - Keep inexpensive silicone dust plugs in unused 3.5 mm and USB ports during dry, windy outdoor festivals.
3. **Static Electricity (ESD Protection):**
   - Dry summer winds and synthetic stage astro-turf carpets generate high-voltage electrostatic charges.
   - Always connect your audio output cable to the stage snake **before** plugging your guitar into the input. The audio cable's ground shield bleeds off static charges safely before you touch the pedal.
4. **Sudden Rain Showers:**
   - Summer festival outdoor stages are notorious for sudden 5-minute rain squalls.
   - Keep a roll of clear heavy-gauge plastic sheeting or a transparent PVC dry-bag in your cable tote. In an emergency, toss the clear plastic directly over the pedalboard: you can still see the LEDs and stomp the footswitches through the clear plastic while keeping water off the electronics.

## 6. Pre-Gig Outdoor Festival Checklist

- [ ] Pedal fully charged indoors prior to load-in.
- [ ] USB charger disconnected during outdoor performance in direct sun.
- [ ] Pedal elevated with rubber feet / riser to maintain bottom chassis airflow.
- [ ] White tape or shaded placement utilized to deflect radiant solar heat.
- [ ] Silicone port plugs inserted into unused jacks on dusty/beach stages.
- [ ] Clear rain cover staged near pedalboard for emergency weather.
